# Why Every Contribution Matters

*Written by Nusaiba Mohammed*

It is easy to take our health for granted when we wake up feeling well everyday. We go to work, we go to school, we make plans, and worry about the other things in life. Therefore, it can be difficult to imagine how quickly life can change when someone is diagnosed with cancer.

Cancer doesn't only take a physical toll. It can affect someone's mental health, their family, their ability to work, and their finances. While a patient is trying to focus on getting better, they may also be worrying about how they can afford the treatment and medications they need.

This is one of the reasons your donations matter so much.

## When Resources Are Limited

One of the hardest parts of supporting people is knowing that sometimes there are more needs than resources.

You may have to think about which patients you can support, what kind of support you can provide, or whether you will have enough resources if an urgent case comes up.

But healthcare shouldn't feel like something we have to put a limit on because we are worried about running out of resources.

A patient's need doesn't become less important because there are other people who need help too.

That is why having a strong community behind organizations that support patients is so important. The more resources we have, the more people we can reach and the more support we can provide.

## We Don't Always See the Financial Side of Cancer

There can be medications, appointments, time away from work, and other expenses that add up quickly. For someone who is already dealing with the emotional weight of cancer, financial stress can make things even harder.

It is easy to say that someone's health should come first, and it should. But financial difficulties can make that much harder for some patients and families.

## You Don't Have to Give a Lot

Sometimes people think that if they can't make a large donation, then there is no point in donating at all. That is not true.

Even a small donation can help. If many people give a small amount each month, those donations can come together to create a much bigger impact.

You can give what you are able to give. It doesn't have to be a large amount to matter. As the saying goes, "little drops of water make a mighty ocean". Small amounts add up.

## You Can Help in Other Ways Too

Donating money isn't the only way to support patients.

You can share a campaign with your friends and family, talk about it on social media, or simply tell someone about the work ACCE is doing. Bringing awareness can connect the organization with people who may be able to donate or offer other forms of support.

You can also volunteer your time.

Sometimes we think of helping as something that requires money, but our time, skills, and ability to spread the word can also be valuable.

The important thing is to do what you can.

## It Takes a Community

No single person can solve every problem, and no organization can support every patient, especially without enough resources.

But when a community comes together, things can change.

One person might donate $10 a month. Someone else might share a campaign. Another person might volunteer their time. Another might make a larger donation when they are able.

At ACCE, our **No Patient Left Behind** campaign is about making sure patients have support when they need it. Your donations help support patients with their treatments and medications, and every contribution helps us reach more people.

## A Final Thought

We never really know how much we have until we see someone struggling without it.

Being healthy gives us the freedom to focus on so many other things in life.

We may not be able to take away someone's diagnosis, but we can choose to make their journey a little easier.

If you are able to donate, consider making a small monthly contribution. If you aren't able to donate right now, share the campaign, volunteer your time, or tell someone else about the work ACCE is doing.

Anything can help. And when enough people choose to help, it can become something much bigger.
